## A. Negative obstacles and terrain geometry

- [Matthies03] L. Matthies, A. Rankin, "Negative obstacle detection by thermal signature," Proc. IEEE/RSJ IROS 2003, vol. 1, pp. 906–913, 2003. https://doi.org/10.1109/IROS.2003.1250744 (JPL PDF: https://www-robotics.jpl.nasa.gov/media/documents/matthies-negobs.pdf). Its Eq. (1) gives the ditch's subtended angle as θ ≈ H·w/(R(R+w)) ≈ H·w/R², which falls as 1/R² (a positive obstacle falls as 1/R). This is the geometry behind our missing-ground detector and seen-distance speed governor.
- [Rankin07] A. L. Rankin, A. Huertas, L. H. Matthies, "Night-time negative obstacle detection for off-road autonomous navigation," Proc. SPIE 6561, Unmanned Systems Technology IX, 656103, 2007. https://doi.org/10.1117/12.720513 (the JPL PDF is titled "Nighttime negative obstacle detection…": https://www-robotics.jpl.nasa.gov/media/documents/spie2007-rankin-6561-2.pdf). Multi-cue stereo detection of ditches. It notes that a ditch's visible width spans only a few pixels at stopping distance, and that its subtended angle falls with the inverse square of range.
- [Rankin11] A. Rankin, A. Huertas, L. Matthies, M. Bajracharya, C. Assad, S. Brennan, P. Bellutta, G. W. Sherwin, "Unmanned ground vehicle perception using thermal infrared cameras," Proc. SPIE 8045, Unmanned Systems Technology XIII, 804503, 2011. https://doi.org/10.1117/12.884349 (JPL PDF: https://robotics.jpl.nasa.gov/media/documents/spie-2011-rankin-final.pdf). The abstract says military UGVs often need a passive-only terrain-perception mode so that they do not emit detectable electromagnetic signals. This justifies our camera-only, no-LiDAR, no-radar design.
- [Labayrade02] R. Labayrade, D. Aubert, J.-P. Tarel, "Real time obstacle detection in stereovision on non flat road geometry through 'v-disparity' representation," IEEE Intelligent Vehicle Symposium (IV 2002), vol. 2, pp. 646–651, 2002. https://doi.org/10.1109/IVS.2002.1188024. The v-disparity ground profile needs no flat-world assumption. It is the first stage of our ground model (`autonomy/perception/ground.py`).
- [Hirschmuller08] H. Hirschmüller, "Stereo processing by semiglobal matching and mutual information," IEEE TPAMI, vol. 30, no. 2, pp. 328–341, 2008. https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.2007.1166. This is Semi-Global Matching. OpenCV `StereoSGBM`, which our stereo front end uses (`autonomy/perception/stereo.py`), implements a modified form of it.

## B. Traversability learning

- [GA-Nav22] T. Guan, D. Kothandaraman, R. Chandra, A. J. Sathyamoorthy, K. Weerakoon, D. Manocha, "GA-Nav: Efficient terrain segmentation for robot navigation in unstructured outdoor environments," IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters, vol. 7, no. 3, pp. 8138–8145, 2022. https://doi.org/10.1109/LRA.2022.3187278 (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04233). It groups RUGD and RELLIS-3D classes into coarse navigability levels, which is the precedent for our 5-class terrain ontology.
- [WVN23] J. Frey, M. Mattamala, N. Chebrolu, C. Cadena, M. Fallon, M. Hutter, "Fast traversability estimation for wild visual navigation," Robotics: Science and Systems (RSS XIX), 2023. https://doi.org/10.15607/RSS.2023.XIX.054 (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08510). It learns vision-only traversability online, self-supervised, on pre-trained ViT features. We contrast it with our offline-trained, Jetson-sized model.
- [Velociraptor24] S. Triest, M. Sivaprakasam, S. Aich, D. Fan, W. Wang, S. Scherer, "Velociraptor: Leveraging visual foundation models for label-free, risk-aware off-road navigation," Proc. 8th Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL 2024), PMLR vol. 270, pp. 4483–4494 (volume published 2025). https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/triest25a.html. It turns VFM and geometry features into costmaps, speedmaps and uncertainty maps with no human labels, which is related work for our risk-aware speed governor.
- [TerrainNet23] X. Meng, N. Hatch, A. Lambert, A. Li, N. Wagener, M. Schmittle, J. Lee, W. Yuan, Z. Chen, S. Deng, G. Okopal, D. Fox, B. Boots, A. Shaban, "TerrainNet: Visual modeling of complex terrain for high-speed, off-road navigation," RSS XIX, 2023. https://doi.org/10.15607/RSS.2023.XIX.103 (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15771). It predicts semantic and elevation terrain maps from cameras alone (RGB plus stereo), showing that camera-only off-road terrain modelling works.

## C. Visual localization

- [KITTI12] A. Geiger, P. Lenz, R. Urtasun, "Are we ready for autonomous driving? The KITTI vision benchmark suite," IEEE CVPR 2012, pp. 3354–3361, 2012. https://doi.org/10.1109/CVPR.2012.6248074. This is the stereo odometry benchmark we evaluate our VO on (sequences 00–10 have ground truth).
- [ORB-SLAM3] C. Campos, R. Elvira, J. J. Gómez Rodríguez, J. M. M. Montiel, J. D. Tardós, "ORB-SLAM3: An accurate open-source library for visual, visual–inertial, and multimap SLAM," IEEE Transactions on Robotics, vol. 37, no. 6, pp. 1874–1890, 2021. https://doi.org/10.1109/TRO.2021.3075644 (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11898). The reference stereo and VI SLAM baseline we position our lighter VO against.
- [Maimone07] M. Maimone, Y. Cheng, L. Matthies, "Two years of visual odometry on the Mars Exploration Rovers," Journal of Field Robotics, vol. 24, no. 3, pp. 169–186, 2007. https://doi.org/10.1002/rob.20184 (JPL PDF: https://www-robotics.jpl.nasa.gov/media/documents/rob-06-0081.R4.pdf). Flight-proven stereo VO with no GNSS, used to detect slip. It is the precedent for pairing VO with an integrity and slip check.
- [Furgale10] P. Furgale, T. D. Barfoot, "Visual teach and repeat for long-range rover autonomy," Journal of Field Robotics, vol. 27, no. 5, pp. 534–560, 2010. https://doi.org/10.1002/rob.20342. Long-range route following with a stereo camera as the only sensor and no GPS, which is the precedent for stereo-only waypoint following.
- [Schmidt25] F. Schmidt, C. Blessing, M. Enzweiler, A. Valada, "Visual-inertial SLAM for unstructured outdoor environments: Benchmarking the benefits and computational costs of loop closing," Journal of Field Robotics, vol. 42, no. 7, pp. 3726–3747, 2025. https://doi.org/10.1002/rob.22581 (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01716). It benchmarks ORB-SLAM3, VINS-Fusion, OpenVINS, Kimera, SVO Pro, HFNet-SLAM and AirSLAM in natural terrain, including on embedded compute. This informs our choice of drift versus compute (VO plus an integrity monitor rather than full loop-closing SLAM).
- [TartanAir20] W. Wang, D. Zhu, X. Wang, Y. Hu, Y. Qiu, C. Wang, Y. Hu, A. Kapoor, S. Scherer, "TartanAir: A dataset to push the limits of visual SLAM," IEEE/RSJ IROS 2020, pp. 4909–4916, 2020. https://doi.org/10.1109/IROS45743.2020.9341801 (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.14338). Photo-realistic synthetic stereo with hard motion, lighting and weather; proposed as a future VO stress test (not downloaded or used yet).

## D. Planning and control

- [MPPI16] G. Williams, P. Drews, B. Goldfain, J. M. Rehg, E. A. Theodorou, "Aggressive driving with model predictive path integral control," IEEE ICRA 2016, pp. 1433–1440, 2016. https://doi.org/10.1109/ICRA.2016.7487277. The sampling-based MPC (MPPI) our local planner is built on.
- [Mandow07] A. Mandow, J. L. Martínez, J. Morales, J. L. Blanco, A. García-Cerezo, J. González, "Experimental kinematics for wheeled skid-steer mobile robots," IEEE/RSJ IROS 2007, pp. 1222–1227, 2007. https://doi.org/10.1109/IROS.2007.4399139. The ICR-based skid-steer kinematics we use to map (v, ω) to left and right wheel commands.

## E. Perception models

- [MobileNetV3] A. Howard, M. Sandler, G. Chu, L.-C. Chen, B. Chen, M. Tan, W. Wang, Y. Zhu, R. Pang, V. Vasudevan, Q. V. Le, H. Adam, "Searching for MobileNetV3," IEEE/CVF ICCV 2019, pp. 1314–1324, 2019. https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCV.2019.00140 (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02244). It introduces the LR-ASPP head. Our terrain model is torchvision `lraspp_mobilenet_v3_large`.
- [FiT3D] Y. Yue, A. Das, F. Engelmann, S. Tang, J. E. Lenssen, "Improving 2D feature representations by 3D-aware fine-tuning," ECCV 2024, LNCS, pp. 57–74, 2024. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-72627-9_4 (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.20229). It fine-tunes DINOv2 features with 3D awareness, which is a possible future upgrade for terrain features.
- [DINOv2] M. Oquab, T. Darcet, T. Moutakanni, H. Vo, et al., "DINOv2: Learning robust visual features without supervision," Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR), 01/2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07193 (OpenReview: https://openreview.net/forum?id=a68SUt6zFt). The foundation features behind WVN, Velociraptor and FiT3D. It is a heavier alternative to our lightweight model.
- [SegFormer] E. Xie, W. Wang, Z. Yu, A. Anandkumar, J. M. Alvarez, P. Luo, "SegFormer: Simple and efficient design for semantic segmentation with transformers," Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 34 (NeurIPS 2021), 2021. https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/64f1f27bf1b4ec22924fd0acb550c235-Abstract.html (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.15203). A transformer segmentation reference point, heavier than LR-ASPP on edge hardware.

## G. Indian context sources

- [DGCA-AC1-2023] Directorate General of Civil Aviation (Govt. of India), "ANSS Advisory Circular AC 1 of 2023, Sub: GNSS Interference in Airspace," Ref. DGCA-21040/1/2023-ANS, dated **24.11.2023** (14 pp.). Copy: https://images.assettype.com/thefourthonline/2023-11/e7cf9f10-2b3b-44ed-ae92-bbf6223711de/DGCA_CIRCULAR.pdf (also at https://ops.group/blog/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/DGCA-Circular-on-GNSS-Spoofing.pdf). It defines GNSS jamming and spoofing as a real airspace threat, sets stakeholder roles, and mandates reporting. This is official evidence that GNSS denial is live in Indian airspace. **[UNVERIFIED] official URL**: the header was checked on this copy, but the dgca.gov.in hosting URL was not found.
- [LS-2025-03-20] Lok Sabha written reply by MoS Civil Aviation Murlidhar Mohol, **20 Mar 2025**. About 465 GPS interference/spoofing incidents were reported in the border region, mostly Amritsar/Jammu, between Nov 2023 and Feb 2025. Source: ETV Bharat (PTI), 20 Mar 2025, https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!bharat/several-airlines-operating-aircraft-in-around-amritsar-report-gps-interference-govt-enn25032004486. This matches the Deccan Herald, Shillong Times and ORF reports. It quantifies GNSS denial near the border, where a BEL UGV would operate. **[UNVERIFIED] primary**: the sansad.in question PDF was not located.
- [RS-2025-12-01] Rajya Sabha reply by Civil Aviation Minister K. Rammohan Naidu (question by S. Niranjan Reddy), **1 Dec 2025**. Some flights reported GPS spoofing while flying GPS-based approaches to **Runway 10 at IGI Delhi**. Contingency procedures were used, and other runway ends were unaffected because they have conventional navaids. Reports also came from Kolkata, Amritsar, Mumbai, Hyderabad, Bengaluru and Chennai, and a DGCA SOP for real-time spoofing reports was issued in Nov 2025. Source: All India Radio News (govt), https://www.newsonair.gov.in/civil-aviation-minister-informs-rajya-sabha-of-gps-spoofing-near-igi-airport-dgca-issues-sop. It shows spoofing reaching the capital and the value of a non-GNSS fallback. The sansad.in/PIB primary text was not retrieved; this government broadcaster report is the source used.
- [NavIC-2026] T. Cozzens, "India's IRNSS-1F satellite fails after atomic clock malfunction," GPS World, **16 Mar 2026**, quoting ISRO. https://www.gpsworld.com/indias-irnss-1f-satellite-fails-after-atomic-clock-malfunction/. The IRNSS-1F on-board atomic clock stopped on **13 Mar 2026**, after the 10-year design life ended on 10 Mar 2026; the satellite now does one-way messaging only. Three satellites still provide PNT: IRNSS-1B, IRNSS-1I and NVS-01. Three is below the four a standalone 3-D fix normally needs (a general GNSS requirement, our inference), so the national fallback (NavIC) is degraded too. **[UNVERIFIED] satellite name**: GPS World prints "IRNSS-1L", while Wikipedia lists IRNSS-1I. The ISRO X post (https://x.com/r_isro/status/2032498043149570556) was not fetched.
- [BEL-RSP] Bharat Electronics Ltd., "Robotic Surveillance Platform," product page (accessed 30 Sep 2026). https://bel-india.in/product/robotic-surveillance-platform/. A UGV with waypoint navigation and remote control, obstacle detection and avoidance, day/night operation, HD video, up to 4 h endurance and **max 3.6 km/h**. This is the problem-owner platform METAGROSS targets.
- [Jetson-ONS] NVIDIA, "Jetson Orin Nano Super Developer Kit," product/spec page. https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/autonomous-machines/embedded-systems/jetson-orin/nano-super-developer-kit/. 67 INT8 TOPS; Ampere GPU with 1024 CUDA and 32 tensor cores; 6-core Arm Cortex-A78AE; 8 GB LPDDR5 at 102 GB/s; 7–25 W. This is our target compute (segmentation + VO + MPPI budget).
- [OAK-D-Lite] Luxonis, "OAK-D Lite," hardware docs. https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/products/OAK-D%20Lite. Two OV7251 global-shutter mono cameras (640×480, HFOV 73°) plus an IMX214 colour camera; **75 mm stereo baseline** (the datasheet says 7.5 cm; the docs page misprints it as "75cm"); ideal depth range about 0.8–12 m; RVC2 at 4 TOPS (1.4 TOPS for AI); BMI270 IMU. This is the low-cost stereo option (hardware tier B). The reference sensor modelled in simulation and in `config/defaults.py` is a ZED 2i-class rig with a 12 cm baseline; with 7.5 cm the depth error grows by 12/7.5 = 1.6× at the same range (see `deck_assets/theory/stereo_depth_error.png`).
